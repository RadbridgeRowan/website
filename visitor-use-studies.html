Visitor use studies

We estimate how many people visit parks, trails, rivers, and public lands, when they come, and where they travel from. Agencies use our visitor use studies to plan staffing, programming, access improvements, and outreach.

What a visitor use study tells you

We work with park agencies, state recreation and natural resource agencies, water agencies, nonprofits, and planning teams, from individual parks to statewide studies.

How many people visit

Visits by site, season, and month, including at sites without counters or entry fees.

Where visitors come from

Visitor origins, travel distances, and the split between local residents and visitors from outside the region.

How use varies

Visit timing, repeat use, and differences across parks, river segments, or access sites.

Who you could reach

Regional markets and recreation interests that point to opportunities for outreach and new services.

How we do it

We share findings through reports, maps, charts, and supporting datasets, or through ongoing dashboard access with Parkbeat.

Mobile location data

Analyze anonymized mobile location data to estimate visitation and visitor origins, from a single site to more than a thousand sites at once.

Your records

Combine mobile data with visitor counts, trail counters, permits, and reservation records to check and scale the estimates.

Surveys and public input

Online and map-based surveys let people identify the places they use and describe access, safety, and facility concerns.

Search and review data

Online search patterns and public reviews add information about recreation interests and the visitor experience.

Common questions

How accurate is mobile location data for estimating visits?

Mobile location data captures a sample of visitors, not every visitor. We check and scale estimates against counts or records from your sites wherever they exist, and we report assumptions and limitations alongside the results.

Is mobile location data private?

We work with anonymized data and report results for sites and areas, not for individuals.

Can a visitor use study support a grant application?

Yes. According to Iowa DNR, the City of Spencer used our Little Sioux River analysis to help secure a $250,000 grant for access improvements.

Can you track visitation over time?

Yes. Parkbeat offers dashboard access with updates as new data become available, so you can follow trends across seasons and years.
