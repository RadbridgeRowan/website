Economic impact analysis

We estimate how parks, trails, waterways, and recreation projects support local businesses, jobs, income, and tax revenue. Public agencies use our economic impact and contribution studies in master plans, funding requests, and public discussions.

What an economic impact study tells you

We work with park agencies, state recreation offices, water and flood agencies, and planning teams at local, regional, and statewide scales across the United States.

How much visitors spend

Estimate visits and visitor spending at lodging, restaurants, retail, and outfitters, and separate local residents from visitors who travel from outside the region.

Jobs, income, and tax revenue

Trace how visitor spending, park operations, and capital investment support jobs, labor income, value added, and state and local tax revenue in your county, region, or state.

What a proposed project could add

Build visitation scenarios for new or improved facilities based on comparable sites, regional population, and travel patterns, and estimate construction effects.

Economic impact or economic contribution?

The two terms are often used interchangeably, but they answer different questions. We help you choose the measure that fits your decision.

Economic contribution

Measures all economic activity associated with a park or recreation system, including spending by local residents. It shows the role a place plays in the local economy today.

Economic impact

Measures only the activity that would not happen without the park or project, such as spending by visitors from outside the region or new visits a proposed facility would attract. It is the right measure for evaluating a new investment.

Why the difference matters

Contribution estimates are usually larger than impact estimates for the same place. Reporting the right one, with sources and assumptions, keeps findings credible with budget officers, funders, and the public.

How we do it

We combine data science with economic analysis and share findings through reports, concise summaries, maps, and web-based tools. Our studies make sources, assumptions, and limitations clear.

Visitation

Combine visitor counts, reservation records, and anonymized mobile location data to estimate visits, visitor origins, and seasonal patterns, including at sites with little existing data.

Visitor spending

Build spending profiles from published research and visitor surveys, matched to activities and trip types.

Regional economic modeling

Use input-output models such as IMPLAN and RIMS II to translate direct spending into jobs, labor income, value added, and supply-chain effects.

Tax revenue

Estimate the state and local tax revenue that recreation spending supports.

Common questions

Can you estimate the impact of a park that hasn’t been built yet?

Yes. For proposed facilities we develop visitation scenarios from comparable sites, regional population, and travel patterns, then estimate spending and construction effects for each scenario. Our St. Anthony Whitewater Park analysis is one example.

What if we don’t have reliable visitor counts?

Many sites don’t. We combine the records you have, such as counters, permits, or reservations, with anonymized mobile location data to estimate visitation and where visitors come from. See visitor use studies.

What area does the analysis cover?

We define the study area with you. It is usually a county, a multi-county region, or a state. Our statewide Nevada study, for example, gives each county its own results.

Does economic impact include the value of recreation itself?

No. Economic impact measures spending and the jobs and income it supports. The value people receive from recreation beyond what they spend is measured separately. See recreation value and public benefits.

Where do you work?

Across the United States. Recent clients include public agencies in California, Idaho, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, and Virginia.
